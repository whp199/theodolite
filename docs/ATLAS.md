# The atlas and its census

*Every feed-forward neuron of Gemma-4-31B-it on one map, and the census that measures what a layer’s neurons do when pushed, as the README told it, with the census’s state layer by layer up to 16 November 2026. The live state is the [census board](https://github.com/whp199/theodolite/issues/1); every figure is in [ROADMAP §vi](../ROADMAP.md#vi--the-atlas).*

Every feed-forward neuron of Gemma-4-31B-it on one map (`#/atlas`): sixty sheets of 21,504, the neurons that write alike
side by side, each layer’s attention heads before its sheet. Open one and the atlas gathers what can be read of it.
Above is neuron 17,966 of layer 47. Through the lens it writes “waves”, “waterfall” and “波”. Its web of weight links runs
back to what drives it (“thread”, “hurricane”, “earthquake”, “storms”) and on to what it drives. On the right are its
**census** answers: what the model says when it is pushed along this neuron’s write direction. Asked who it is, and
pushed up by the stream’s usual length, the model answers “I am a river wave of same-ocean water, a wind-driven rain of
the same-sea rain”. Left alone, or pushed down, it answers “I am a large language model, trained by Google.” At twice
that push its text breaks into “Rain Rain Rain”, as text pushed that far along a random direction breaks too.

**The census** is what the atlas measures. While the model answers six fixed questions, each neuron of a layer is pushed
both ways, by once and by twice the stream’s usual length. Its answers are set against those of 2,048 random directions
pushed as far: 565,254 answers a layer, about thirteen hours on one RTX 5090. A neuron ranks high when the words its push
brings stand further above the random directions’ than nearly every random direction’s own words do. So a high rank says
its push differs from a random one. It does not say that its words share a theme, or name what the neuron does when it
fires. The doses were first calibrated at layers 41–51. The census covers those eleven layers and, since 16 to 19
November 2026, eleven more: 32–40, 52 and 53 (below).

All eleven are in: layers 41–51 (40–50 on the [census board](https://github.com/whp199/theodolite/issues/1), which
counts from 0), six of them taken by volunteers. Layer 46 was read against gates fixed before it ran, and the other ten
against the same gates after the fact. Every gate passes on all eleven. Read at 48 word-pieces, between 3,534 and 9,133
of a layer’s 21,504 neurons have a false-discovery q of 0.05 or less, and between 95 and 4,794 beat all 2,048 random
directions, where about ten would by chance (a count that rests on each layer’s one strongest random direction). How
each was taken: [VOLUNTEERS.md](../VOLUNTEERS.md).

A second round is taking the other forty-nine. A layer outside 41–51 is not taken blind: about a thousand of its
neurons are trialled first on one card, some 80 minutes, against gates fixed before any trial ran, and the layer is
offered to volunteers only if it passes. On 12 November 2026 the first two read, layers 40 and 52 (39 and 51 on the
board), passed and were opened, and the doses are counted calibrated at both since that day. Layers 39 and 53 (38 and
52 on the board) failed, from opposite sides: at 39 the same push is three times the most a neuron there can write,
and the text breaks; at 53 it is barely once, and half the answers hardly move. So with these doses the census is
layers 40–52. For the layers outside, two designs that count the push in what a neuron can write by itself were
trialled on 13 November 2026 (ROADMAP §vi). One with two fixed counts passes at layers 39 and 53 (38 and 52 on the
board) and fails at 38. One whose two counts are set a layer at a time, where the next word-piece moves by a fixed
amount, passes at layers 33–38 (32–37 on the board) and fails at 32. The doses are counted calibrated at those eight
layers, and a third kit offers them to volunteers beside layers 40 and 52, each with the design that passed there
([VOLUNTEERS.md](../VOLUNTEERS.md)); layer 37 (36 on the board), which passed one check by a thousandth, was held for the
home card and taken there. Since 14 November 2026 (kit 4) a 24 GB card with nothing else on it can take a layer. A
design that asks twelve questions at one push, the lower of the layer's two counts, passed a trial at layer 32 (31 on
the board) on 16 November and failed at 31; since 17 November kit 5 offers it at 32. At 31 (30 on the board) two
designs failed, and a third passed its trial on 18 November: both counts, on twelve other questions, each asking how
a thing works, for three tips or about an object. Kit 6 offers it there; its doses are not yet counted calibrated.
The same design passed at 54 and 55 (53 and 54 on the board) on 18 and 19 November and failed at 30 and 56; since 19
November kit 7 offers it at 54 and 55, its doses not counted calibrated there either. The other thirty-five layers
(1–30 and 56–60) have no design that passed there. Where a census’s doses are not counted calibrated the atlas
marks it “doses not calibrated at this layer” and inks it without gilt.

Eleven layers of the second round are in. Three volunteers took layers 35, 36, 40 and 52 (34, 35, 39 and 51 on the
board) on 14 and 15 November 2026, and each was accepted on 16 November; layer 52 came from an RTX 3090, the first
layer taken on a 24 GB card. The home card took layer 37 (36) on 14 November. Each was read whole by its own trial’s
reader and three gates. The four volunteers’ layers pass all three. Layer 37 passes two, and on the third its gentler
push’s words, picked on the second half of the questions and scored on the first, lead by 0.564 where 0.57 is asked:
by its trial’s standard it fails, by 0.006. On the owner’s word it is counted done, with that miss named on the
board; whether its doses stay counted calibrated is still the owner’s to decide, and nothing has been changed. Below
layer 41 these layers say less of single neurons. At 35–37 none has a q of 0.05 or less, and 18 to 110 beat all 2,048 random directions: there the
strongest random directions bring the words of another language (Portuguese, Spanish, French, German, Indonesian)
with the text unbroken, and even the fifth strongest stands further above the rest than at any other layer read
whole. At 40, 436 have q ≤ 0.05 and 87 beat all 2,048. Layer 52 reads as 41–51 do: 7,145 and 2,123. On 17 November two
more came from volunteers and pass all three: layer 38 (37 on the board), from an RTX 5090 with the home card's bits,
and layer 34 (33), from an RTX PRO 6000. Unlike 35–37 they say much of single neurons: 2,332 and 5,576 have q ≤ 0.05,
and 454 and 1,041 beat all 2,048. Later that day layer 53 (52) came home from the volunteer who took 52, on the same
RTX 3090, and passes all three too, close to its trial: the whole lead is 0.804 as there, and the thinnest margin is
the gentler push's lead on the new questions alone, 0.566 against a bar of 0.56 (0.577 in the trial). It reads as
41–52 do: 6,933 have q ≤ 0.05 and 3,944 beat all 2,048, and its strongest random direction stands 4.5 of their
deviations out, where at 35–38 even the fifth stood 7.2 or more. On 18 November layer 33 (32 on the board) came
home from the RTX PRO 6000 that took 34–36, and passes all three: the whole lead is 0.714 (0.699 in its trial), and
the words carry by 0.626 both ways. It says much of single neurons: 2,946 have q ≤ 0.05 and 1,245 beat all 2,048,
and its strongest random direction stands 6.1 of their deviations out, writing English, not another language. On 19
November layers 39 and 32 (38 and 31 on the board) came home and pass their trials read whole. 39, from the RTX 5090
that took 38 and 40, bit for bit, leads by 0.694 (0.675 in its trial) and says much of single neurons: 7,003 have q
≤ 0.05, though its strongest random direction stands 19.1 deviations out, writing Portuguese. 32, from the RTX PRO
6000 that took 33–36, asked twelve questions at one push, leads by 0.641 (0.630), its words carrying by 0.584 and
0.586, and 1,893 have q ≤ 0.05. Layer 31 (30) is being taken, and 54 and 55 (53 and 54) are open. Every figure is
in ROADMAP §vi.
