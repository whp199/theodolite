# Judgement under one lens: fit for the owner and the project

Written 14 November 2026. I read both digests and all five designs in full, and checked the claims I lean on against
the repository at commit e6ac64f (read only; nothing run on the card, nothing changed). "Checked" below means I opened
the file named. Layers are counted from 0 unless a page is named.

The lens: one person, one card, a public instrument whose recorded direction is a general-purpose interpretability
tool and learning aid, whose selling point is the atlas and census, and whose habits are plain words, registration
before measurement, and reviews that question the core measurement. Six questions for each design: is the first
increment useful to a person with no agent; does it add to the atlas or compete with it; can one person keep it; is
there an honest public claim at the end and what would make it true; what will age badly; are the owner's decisions
put clearly.

## Scores and ranking

| Rank | Design | Score | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | validity (The Proof Room) | 7.5 | The owner's own method turned into the feature, person first, with a stop gate; heavy to keep, and its names and one seal are wrong |
| 2 | kernel (Levelling) | 7 | The smallest true thing, proved on the card first; says nothing to a learner or the public for weeks and never states a claim |
| 3 | future (The Plane Table) | 6.5 | The only design with a page, a replay, the volunteers and an honest account of "first"; spends its first weeks fixing six standards before one edit is known to be heard |
| 4 | speed (The Proof Press) | 5.5 | Keeps the instrument up and adds real reading tools; by its own sums it speeds up the part that is not slow, and its remedy is several paid drivers at once |
| 5 | driver (Traverse) | 5 | The most literal answer to the ask and the clearest single decision; takes the card from the instrument for hours, builds a third agent surface, and its first increment edits nothing |

No design scores 8. Each would need a second pass before it met the owner.

## What the convergence already settles

All five reach the same core without being told to: an edit is a value (a hashed, saved, additive difference from the
shipped tensor), never a state of the server; it rides some rows of one forward as an added term on a linear's output
(the arithmetic of `backend/int4_cpu.py:138-164`); the unedited row and matched random edits share that forward; the
4-bit grid is a separate, later, measured step; "kept the base" is an ordinary result; the first thing to reproduce is
the 16 October 2026 deletion at layer 50 (-1.80 log-odds, measured). I checked that record:
`experiments/results/E6_tool_audit_04_vault.partial.json` gives -1.799 for the ten 'she' neurons and -0.02 for ten
random neurons on the female contexts, and the neuron list the kernel design quotes is the file's. A synthesis need not
argue any of this again. What the designs differ on is where it lives, who it is for first, and how much is built
before the card has said whether a column edit is heard at all.

## Design by design

### validity (7.5)

- **A person with no agent.** Best of the five. Phase 1 (3 to 5 days, the designer's estimate) is a paired reading by
  command line; Phase 2 ends with "a person can get a verdict they can trust in minutes"; the agent's tools come third.
- **The atlas.** Adds to it. The habitat battery reads side effects on the stored passages where the edited neurons
  fire hardest ("only this instrument can run this battery"), the other-site nulls are matched on how hard neurons
  fire on the very items, and the trust card "limits stories, not effects". It runs in the server and gives the lock
  back between plates, so the pages stay up.
- **One person.** Doubtful as costed. The warden is a second instrument (briefs, generators, fresh draws, two null
  families, four batteries, cheaper-knob arms, rule presets, seven verdicts, a job runner) put at 8 to 12 days. Every
  new operation needs a conformance check, two null generators and a refusal list, written and reviewed by the same
  one person. The owner also writes every brief (generator, near-miss generator, prompt sentences, question bank).
- **The claim.** No project-level claim is stated, but the per-result sentence the warden fills is the most honest
  wording any design offers, down to "not compared with a trained adapter", and a trial of record is the house's own
  registration.
- **Ages badly.** The fixed vocabulary, as drivers get stronger (the design says so and accepts it). The names (below).
- **Decisions.** The clearest set. The first is the right one: whether to build past Phase 1 once the audibility
  pilot (its pilot 5) has run.
- **Strongest:** person-first order with a measured stop gate. **Weakest:** weight of the warden on one maintainer,
  plus names that say what the thing is not.

### kernel (7)

- **A person with no agent.** Yes, for the owner as researcher: one hypothesis about the 31B tested in seconds with
  its controls in the same forward, and the question "is it neuron 5313 alone?" answered in the same call. Nothing
  for a learner: no page until a plain table in increment 4.
- **The atlas.** Reads it, never stales it, does not add to it. In increments 1 and 2 the instrument is down while
  the lab daemon holds the card.
- **One person.** Easily: about 1,550 lines in five files by the design's own count, no new process.
- **The claim.** Not addressed. The design says how the kernel should be judged, not what the project may say.
- **Ages badly.** Little. The lab daemon is the weak plank: unused since 17 October 2026, no cancel, no time limit,
  a health route that stalls (code digest); the design changes only its unpacker.
- **Decisions.** Nine, plain. Missing: a stated rule for stopping after the pilots.
- **Strongest:** pilots that each name the result that would change the design, with the tolerance fixed before the
  known answer is run. **Weakest:** it is a first phase and a sketch of the rest; "environment" is an attended Claude
  Code session, which the owner has today.

### future (6.5)

- **A person with no agent.** Increment 1 is a library and a command line, as the others', but much of it is format
  (manifest, normal form, three hashes, source against lowered payload, round trip). The page arrives in increment 2,
  read-only: a person watches an agent there and cannot pull a reading from it.
- **The atlas.** Adds to it for a reader: the erratum reader shows the neurons an edit touches with their labels
  where the gate allows, and every atlas fact handed on says it describes the base.
- **One person.** The largest: by its own table roughly 9 to 13 weeks. The "unbuilt until needed" table is the right
  device for one maintainer, but the "fixed now" list is still six standards.
- **The claim.** The only design that answers the question (its section xii) and the only one to say that "first" is
  an absence claim from a search, one of which the research already got wrong. Its table of what is not established
  (xiv) is the house habit applied to the design itself.
- **Ages badly.** Version-1 formats fixed before use; docket fields shaped to benchmarks that are announced and not
  released; an object protocol for families that have no builder, over a runtime the code digest calls pinned and
  31B-shaped.
- **Decisions.** Ten, clear. The largest one is not on the list: whether to fix standards before the pilots.
- **Strongest:** the page with rubrics and replay, the volunteers re-running a reading, and the honest account of
  the claim. **Weakest:** standards before evidence.

### speed (5.5)

- **A person with no agent.** Phase 1 is a plan file run by command line: useful. The reading tools that would serve a
  person most (a direction scan of all 60 layers in well under a second, by its arithmetic) come in phase 4, after
  the agent.
- **The atlas.** Adds to it: the tensor register and direction scans fill gaps the code digest lists (no spectral
  index, nothing over attention weights), and the lock is held one forme at a time so pages stay live.
- **One person.** The hardest to keep valid: a row-edit kernel, a span runner that stops at a layer, a layer-by-layer
  backward, a generation-free "parting" proxy, untested grid rules, a cost-model scheduler, a register. Each is a
  core measurement a house review must question.
- **The claim.** One real candidate: survival on the QAT grid read as one more row of the same forward. It rests on
  derivations the design marks untested (its pilot 8).
- **Ages badly.** Several drivers at once; a backward pass as the default first step (results then cannot sit beside
  the gradient-free published work); "fast registration" that carries the word "confirmed".
- **Decisions.** Ten, clear, and it says plainly where it leaves the house habit.
- **Strongest:** the cost model from four dated records, logged against actual seconds and refitted, with a free
  estimate before any spend. **Weakest:** it finds the card busy 2 to 40% of the time and still spends most of its
  design on the card.

### driver (5)

- **A person with no agent.** Weakest. Phase 1 "needs no new edit machinery at all": it is the lab daemon made safe,
  plus a ledger. The owner's recent experiments already run as registered scripts of their own.
- **The atlas.** Competes for the card: a session holds it for three hours by default with the server down, and the
  results live in a workspace no page reads until phase 4. The scientist is "untouched, and not the driver's loop",
  so the project would have three agent surfaces.
- **One person.** About 3,050 lines by its own count in a separate system (keeper, two workers, three faces), a
  sandbox with a GPU that is itself a pilot, and a check to re-run at every CLI release.
- **The claim.** A known-answer positive and negative control as the bench's first verdict is the right first public
  fact. Nothing further is stated.
- **Ages badly.** CLI flags, sandbox details, three faces. `SCAFFOLD.md` with the assumption behind each convenience
  is the best answer to ageing in any design.
- **Decisions.** Eleven, and the first is the crispest sentence in the five: whether driver code may run on the card
  at all.
- **Strongest:** state in files and a thin harness whose every convenience names the assumption that would retire
  it; a machine-wide card lock. **Weakest:** the instrument goes dark while the feature is used.

**future**
- Its sections xii and xiv: what the claim needs, and what the design rests on that is not established.

**speed**

## What all five missed

1. **Codex reads the disk at the default reach.** `README.md:694` says so. Every seal that is a file (held items, the
   ledger, briefs) fails against one of the three backends the scientist already has. Three designs state the
   opposite. The choice is the owner's: fresh draws only, a separate account, or sealed tiers for Claude Code and
   endpoint drivers alone.
2. **The person's first surface already exists and none of them uses it.** The vault page has an edit form with a
   plain-words preview and a before-and-after hearing; the push tab has a three-voice thread. Base, edit and a chance
   edit heard side by side in one batch is the natural second increment and the learning aid. All five leave the
   vault as it is and only refuse to run beside it.
3. **Faults in default-visible readings that the code digest found today are left standing.** After a vault edit the
   stats index and the whole census are served with no stale flag; steer-lens layers 53 to 59 are shown as usable
   though no census checks them; the vault clamps layer 99 to layer 59; an overlay on an attention linear survives
   ROLLBACK; the README says 30 tools where the code has 35; R4's pin on `engine_runtime.py` has not held since 12
   November 2026. The designs mention the pin and the clamp. None schedules the repairs before building on top.
4. **Nothing is written back to the atlas.** Thirty-nine of sixty layers have no calibrated push dose and no causal
   reading with a null. Scaling a neuron's own write column by 0 or 2, read on its stored passages against matched
   columns, needs no dose. If the audibility pilots show such edits are heard, that is an atlas product for exactly
   the layers the census cannot reach. If they show they are not, that too is a fact about the atlas. (My inference;
   its cost is not measured.)
5. **The roadmap's one weight-space item is not mentioned.** `ROADMAP.md:133-134`: the base model against the instruct
   one, as the nearest thing to watching a neuron form. A column of the base laid on as a delta is the same
   machinery with no agent. The base weights are not in the Hugging Face cache on this machine (checked today), so it
   needs a download and the owner's word.
6. **No dollar ceiling for the driver.** The house budget rule has the owner approve hours and dollars ahead. The
   designs log tokens and dollars; none estimates a session or asks for a ceiling. Public figures for one run range
   from about $15 to $312 (research digest).
7. **No lane on the real weights while the card is busy.** A census layer holds the card for 13.2 hours. Four designs
   say sessions must be scheduled; future offers a 12B that has no atlas. The CPU int4 kernel already lays an edit on
   as a difference term on the 31B, at 0.45 s a word-piece and about 21 GB from the QAT Q4_0 GGUF (measured 2
   November 2026; the checkpoint's own figures are not recorded). It is another numeric class and would be labelled
   so. Whether it slows a running census is not established.
8. **Who else can run it.** A volunteer's headless 24 GB card holds the model (17.97 GiB at rest with the lens freed,
   measured 14 November 2026). No design states the feature's memory floor for that card.
9. **The size of "first".** A public harness already runs a 27B model in a 4-bit format in 19.6 GB on an RTX 5090,
   and weight edits of this very 31B exist (abliteration builds; a backdoor study on the bf16 release), all as the
   research digest records them. "First on a
   31B" would be true by model, not by kind. What would be new in kind: a causal atlas as the symbol table with its
   trust bands stated; every number paired in one forward with matched random edits; time and cost per test
   published; the grid measured. The repository's references do not yet cite the paper, and the claim needs a dated
   search on record, worded as `VOLUNTEERS.md:51` words the atlas's ("we found none published").
10. **The house's own gates are not in the estimates.** Every effort figure is build days. None counts the review
    with a validity reader for each increment, the Lexicon entries, the ROADMAP section or the REFERENCES entries.

## Advice to the synthesiser

1. Take kernel's size and pilots, validity's order and guards, future's page and its account of the claim. Leave
   speed's backward sweep, register and several drivers, driver's keeper and faces, future's object protocol and
   exchange formats, and most of validity's warden in a table of things unbuilt until a named need arrives.
2. Run the pilots as a plain script with the server stopped, as the census jobs are run, not by reviving the lab
   daemon. Move into the server, one forward a lock, as soon as the bit checks pass, so the instrument stays up.
3. Make the first owner decision validity's: build further or stop, on the audibility pilot. Fix its threshold
   before it runs.
4. Make the second increment a person's: three voices on the page (base, edit, chance edit), with rubrics. Then the
   agent, through the scientist's one registry, typed. Free code stays the owner's own attended session until a
   boundary at the operating system exists; put driver's first decision to the owner in driver's words.
5. Correct the reach facts before any design of seals. Default to fresh draws.
6. Do not call anything a proof or an erratum. "Sighting" is taken. Count the new words and keep them few.
7. State the claim the way future does, add the 27B analogue to it, and say what makes it true: a registered known
   answer, a recorded session with its ledger and its cost, a volunteer's re-run.
8. Put a short list of repairs before the feature, and the dollar ceiling beside the card-time budget.
9. Fix no format before an edit is known to be heard. Version a small manifest and stop there.

## What I did not check

I ran nothing on the card and started no server. I did not open the papers; the literature is taken from the research
digest with its corrections. I did not read the six research or nine code reports end to end, only the lines I cite.
Effort figures are each designer's own and I did not re-estimate them. Whether bubblewrap can hold the card on this
machine's kernel, whether the 12B builds, and whether any single-column edit is heard on the 31B remain as each
design left them: unmeasured.
