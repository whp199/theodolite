## wsp_paper — The weight-space programming paper itself, read in full
Full report: experiments/results/weight_space_exploration/research/wsp_paper.md

### Checker's verdict
The report can be trusted on its primary-source facts: I opened the blog, the logs repository files it cites and all 158 per-turn usage records of the 50-pass run, and almost every number I recomputed matched exactly (benchmarks, edit formula and size, turn and pass counts, handoff contents, prompts, notices, Go and Gemma tables, the model card and the coverage pages; no source was missing or misidentified, and no page contained instructions aimed at an AI). The weaknesses are in interpretation. One design implication rests on a claim the logs contradict (25 passes spent finding the direction; it took three). Two inferences are stronger than the evidence (that naming the failure in the prompt was what mattered, and that the explain-every-step rule shaped the result). The token total is about 16% low because of a counter fault the researcher did not notice, and that same fault hides the most design-relevant event in the logs: the agent ran 26 turns with its conversation frozen and survived on its notebook and work folder. Finally, the statements about open-weights drivers and scale are true only of the original post; follow-up papers linked from the post itself, which the researcher did not open, already cover open-weight editors and targets of 2B to 9B parameters, and I read those only as abstracts. The check is appended to experiments/results/weight_space_exploration/research/wsp_paper.md under "## Independent citation check"; the original text is byte-for-byte unchanged.

### CORRECTIONS (these override the takeaways below)
- The researcher's token total for the 50-pass Qwen run (5.44M input, 149k output) is an undercount: the first conversation's cumulative counter stops growing after turn 14 (303,607 at turn 14, then about 337,900 at every turn through 40), so 26 turns of about 34,000 input tokens each are missing, and the corrected total is about 6.29M input (6.02M cached) and about 162k output.
- The researcher attributed the state-recovery turns 16 to 25 to a kernel restart, but the released records show a larger fault: from turn 15 to turn 40 the Codex session store failed to write ("Quota exceeded" in every turn's stderr) and per-turn input stayed flat at 34,000 to 34,600 tokens, so the model was seeing the conversation as of turn 14 plus the newest message; this also explains six of the eight 'sent back unrun' replies (turns 31 to 39) and the duplicated 32-problem requests 8/9 and 10/11.
- The researcher said 19 of 69 forward requests failed for infrastructure reasons, but passes.md shows 16 generation-engine start failures, 2 attention-output bugs and 1 error by the agent itself (an unknown practice id).
- The researcher used the shorter prompt's failure to submit an edit at 10 passes as evidence that naming the failure in the prompt mattered, but the same results table shows that prompt scoring 14.1 / 25.1 at 25 passes (identical to the longer prompt), its unsubmitted 10-pass candidate scoring 10.1 / 16.1, and the longer prompt also declining to submit at 5 passes.
- The researcher wrote that Astra spent roughly its first 25 passes rediscovering a direction and its 64 writer neurons, but the logs show the direction and the writer data came from counted passes 2 and 3, and the agent's notebook says the 64 writers were chosen only from that third pass; passes 4 to 32 were causal testing.
- The researcher wrote that the paper relied on an 872,000-token window, but 872,000 is a configuration value the harness passed to Codex; single-turn input in Experiment 8 peaked at about 83,000 tokens and the largest single prompt in the release is 355,201 tokens.
- The researcher called Simon Willison's test 'the one open-weights data point', but IWIBench (arXiv 2611.08647), linked from the blog's own Response to Comments and not opened by the researcher, states in its abstract that it evaluated closed- and open-weight editor models editing a Gemma 3 4B IT target, with Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol far ahead.
- The researcher wrote that only the Go sandbox is released as harness code, but the Experiment 7 sandbox prelude (prelude.py) and Experiment 8's evaluation, checkpoint-build and edit-diff scripts are also in the repository; what is missing is the Experiment 8 agent loop, kernel and forward-pass server.
- The researcher wrote that attention patterns on 16 self-written texts gave 3 of 3, but the limit is 16 texts per attention call and Astra made 4 or 5 calls per pair.
- The researcher wrote that the prompt 'then warns' about printing vocabulary tokens after the content-filter refusal, but that warning is already in the first message (turns/t001/prompt.txt), and the harness's own notice names two possible triggers (raw vocabulary tokens or long reasoning transcripts).
- The researcher wrote that the authors conclude editing can remove an obstacle but not add an ability, but the blog's sentence is in the past tense about the Gemma 3 270M experiment only, and the same post's outlook still expects capabilities to be added by prompt.
- The researcher reported an arXiv follow-up with sixteen editing trajectories as unverified, but it exists as arXiv 2611.09382 (Hu and others, 6 November 2026, Qwen3.5-0.8B and Qwen3.5-9B, six of 16 trajectories keeping the original weights).

### Takeaways
- The original 'Sparks of Weight-Space Programming' is a blog post (2 November 2026) with a logs repository, not an arXiv paper, and the repository releases prompts, transcripts, networks and evaluations but only one piece of harness code (the Go sandbox); the Experiment 8 kernel and forward-pass server exist only as described in prompts.
- The harness is deliberately primitive: gpt-6-astra is called one turn at a time through the Codex CLI with every built-in tool disabled and a one-line system prompt, and it acts by replying with exactly one fenced block (```python, ```forward, ```games, ```positions or ```attention) that the authors' loop executes and answers with truncated printed output (10,000 to 20,000 characters).
- Raw weights were shown as text only up to 171,664 weights (about 2 to 4.3 tokens per weight, 355,201 input tokens for the largest); from 1.5M weights upward the model saw weights only through load(name)/save(name) helpers and the summaries its own code printed.
- The Qwen3.5-0.8B headline (MATH-500 7.9 -> 48.1) is an activation-led result: Astra built a difference-of-means 'repetition' direction from four matched teacher-forced contexts, found the MLP neurons whose down_proj columns write it, and applied W = W0 - 6*outer(u, mask64*(u@W0)) to layers 15 and 19 (131,072 values, 8 to 11% Frobenius change of each matrix), which is directional ablation written into weights and over-driven sixfold.
- The Experiment 8 task prompt itself named the failure that became the fix (a model that repeats itself and never finishes) and taught the method (causal localisation, dose-response, paired comparisons, SE of about 50/sqrt(n), held-out confirmation); a shorter prompt version without the failure example produced no submitted edit at 10 passes.
- Forward passes, not weight reading, were the decisive resource at language-model scale: on Qwen3.5-0.8B 0 and 5 passes yielded no edit, 10 and 25 passes yielded MATH-500 of 12.3 and 14.1, and only 50 passes yielded 48.1; on Gemma 3 270M weights alone failed to identify the sycophantic fine-tune in four attempts while attention patterns on 16 self-written texts gave 3 of 3.
- The forward-pass tool's best idea is the paired 'both' pass, which runs original and edited weights on the same items with per-item seeds and returns the accuracy difference with its standard error and rescue/regression counts; its worst flaw was that separate generation passes were not reproducible because server batching changed the random numbers.
- The 50-pass Qwen run cost 158 turns, 80 Python runs, 69 forward requests (19 failed for infrastructure reasons), 20.3 hours wall-clock and, by my sum of the released usage records, about 5.4M cumulative input tokens (95% cached) and 149k output tokens; LLM turns took 20 to 70 seconds, so generation passes of up to half an hour dominated the time, and no dollar cost is stated.
- Memory across turns was a Codex thread restarted every 40 turns with the task, counters, the list of edited tensors, a /work listing and the last 8,000 characters of the agent's own notes.md; the agent additionally kept named backups of candidate edits, exact next-pass plans in JSON, frozen-weight hashes and an archived experiment history.
- Edit forms that worked were structured and sparse: index/slice assignments that take over a few units per layer (Kinetix, 3.5% of weights), sparse from-zero writes (MinAtar, 3 to 4% nonzero), a construction script (Go) and a column-masked rank-one removal (Qwen); single-neuron ablations, head halving and column or head rescaling did not produce reliable gains.
- The Gemma 3 270M follow-up is the paper's most important negative: where failures were ordinary arithmetic and algebra errors with no single component behind them, 5 to 50 passes moved MATH-500 from 6.8 to at most 7.7, and the authors conclude editing can remove an obstacle but not add an ability.
- Held-out evaluation was real for the final numbers (MATH-500, GSM8K test, IFEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA never shown to the agent), but every side-effect benchmark was run under the same looping condition and therefore improved, so the paper contains no measurement of collateral damage that is independent of the bug being fixed.
- The authors used only one closed driver model and say nothing specific about scaling beyond 0.8B parameters or about quantised models; their only stated limits are context length and harness inefficiency, and the one open-weights data point (Qwen 3.8 27B naming 3 of 4 toy games at about an hour each) comes from outside coverage.

### Design implications (the researcher's, unjudged)
- Offer the agent the paper's proven minimal protocol (one fenced code or request block per turn, printed output capped near 10,000 characters, a persistent work folder, a notes file re-shown on restart) alongside MCP tools, because it needs no tool-calling support and so lets any current or future driver model, open or closed, run the loop.
- Give weight access only through lazy named-tensor helpers that dequantise one tensor or slice on demand and never hand out a whole-model float copy: one 5,376 x 21,504 feed-forward matrix is 115.6M entries (462 MB as float32, versus 14.7 MB for the Qwen matrices Astra edited), and the full 31B model in float32 is about 124 GB.
- Precompute and cache the per-tensor statistics the paper's agent recomputed by hand each run (column norms, top singular vectors, direction-times-matrix products for every feed-forward output matrix) so that a single agent turn can scan all 60 layers within its time limit.
- Make contrastive teacher-forced reads the default measurement (token log-probs, residuals, feed-forward activations and attention on agent-written matched texts) and treat generation as the expensive exception, because on Qwen3.5-0.8B the cheap teacher-forced passes carried the selectivity, dose-response and site-isolation evidence while generation passes took up to half an hour each on a rented GPU.
- Represent every edit as an overlay on the frozen 4-bit base (rank-one factors plus a column mask, sparse entries, or column zeroing) with sha256 of base and result, since one 32 GB card cannot hold an original and an edited 31B model at once and the paper's own diff format already stores edits this way.
- Implement the paired original-versus-edited comparison by applying and reverting the overlay inside one request with identical per-item seeds and identical batch composition, and return the difference with its standard error and wrong-to-right / right-to-wrong counts as the paper's 'both' pass does.
- Make runs bit-reproducible across requests and memoise the unedited model's result for each (item, seed, decoding) key, which the paper's server could not do, so that every paired comparison after the first costs one model run instead of two.
- Budget the agent in GPU-seconds or tokens and show the remaining balance each turn rather than counting passes, because pass-counting led the paper's agents to waste budget on 32-item batches with 4 to 7 point standard errors until the experimenters told them size was free.
- Accept a list of candidate overlays in one request and return a table (several strengths, each site alone, a norm-matched random-direction control), because the 50-pass run spent about fifteen separate passes on exactly those sweeps.
- Have the harness own a sealed confirmation set that an agent can spend only on a frozen, hashed edit, and enforce a dev / hold-out split from the start, instead of leaving held-out discipline to the agent as prompt version 3 did.
- Ship an off-target battery that is independent of the behaviour being edited (divergence on neutral text, unrelated task accuracy, other behaviours of the touched neurons from the atlas) and report it with every edit, because the paper's side-effect benchmarks all shared the loop pathology and could only go up.
- Refuse or flag degenerate edits at the save step as the paper's save_edit did (final norm, embedding and unembedding, whole-tensor scalar multiples), and record relative Frobenius change, changed rows/columns and off-direction fraction automatically for every saved edit.
- Re-measure any accepted edit in the form that will ship: the paper evaluated float edits cast to bfloat16, whereas on a W4A16 checkpoint an edit is either a float overlay exported as an adapter or is re-quantised to the 4-bit grid, where small changes round away and large ones pick up rounding error.
- Expose the existing causally tested neuron atlas and intervention tools as first-class queries (which neurons write along this direction, what happens if this set is ablated at this dose), because Astra spent roughly its first 25 passes rediscovering a direction and its 64 writer neurons per layer.
- Put the methods brief in the task prompt (state the hypothesis before each step, show dose-response, compare paired on the same items, quote the standard error, keep an edit only if it holds on unused items) and send unexplained actions back unrun, since those prompt rules visibly shaped the successful 50-pass trajectory.
- Design for failure of everything around the agent: 19 of 69 forward requests failed, kernels restarted and lost variables, a provider content filter refused a turn that printed raw vocabulary tokens, and the model API was unreachable for 4.7 hours, so requests must be idempotent and free on failure, state must live on disk, and printed output must be filtered to token ids and short ASCII excerpts.
- Set expectations and metrics around targeted removal, suppression or rerouting of identified features with measured specificity, not around benchmark jumps, because the paper's only large language-model gain removed a pathology (93% of answers looping) and its attempt to add ability to a non-looping model gained nothing.
- Do not build the harness around a specific context size or vendor: the paper relied on an 872,000-token window, 40-turn handoffs and cumulative inputs of 1.8M to 8.1M tokens per run, so keep the per-turn payload small and the durable state (notes, plan, candidate edits, results table) outside the conversation.

### Open questions
- Does the paper's activation-contrast then column-masked rank-one recipe transfer to a healthy 31B model that has no dominant pathology, or does it only work when one direction explains most failures as in Qwen3.5-0.8B?
- How large must an overlay be to survive re-quantisation to a 4-bit grid, and does a float overlay beside W4A16 weights behave the same as the paper's bfloat16-cast edits?
- How much of the 50-pass result came from the prompt naming repetition as the failure, given that prompt version 4 without the example submitted no edit at 10 passes and that only one run per budget exists?
- Can an open-weights driver model run the tool-using loop (Experiments 6 to 8) at all? The only open-weights evidence is no-tools game identification on 17,253 weights at about an hour per answer.
- What generation length and batch size make a paired pass on a 31B 4-bit model statistically useful within minutes on one 32 GB card, given that the paper's passes assumed up to 512 answers of up to 32,768 tokens on a rented GPU?
- Would allowing gradients for attribution (which the authors say the concept does not rule out) change the cost-benefit on a single card, and can they be computed at all through a W4A16 checkpoint within 32 GB?
- The results table shows the 25-pass prompt-v4 run with exactly the same scores (14.1 / 25.1) as the 25-pass prompt-v3 run; is that a coincidence, the same edit found twice, or a table error?
- What will the authors' announced benchmark and harness look like, and will it define a standard request format for forward passes and edits that a new harness should match?

### Takeaways
- The Qwen3.5-0.8B edit itself reproduces: at least five independent parties re-scored the published diff at 46.8-51.7% on MATH-500 against the authors' 48.1% (HF rebuild 51.0%, ajaygrover 46.8%, Beaker 51.1%, Barry 50.9%, Tree-Ising 51.7%).
- The headline gain is loop repair, not new ability: in the authors' own logs 576 of 579 rescued practice answers had been cut off at the 32,768-token cap, accuracy on the 114 already-finished problems stayed at 93.9%, and the same procedure on Gemma 3 270M (which does not loop) moved MATH-500 only from 6.8 to 7.0-7.7.
- The 7.9% baseline was a settings artefact: Toledo's pre-registered re-run of the unedited model with thinking off at the model card's settings scored 44.0% (floor) to 49.7% (decided), and two Hacker News commenters reported 46% and 52%, so the cheapest baseline nearly matches the edit.
- An LLM agent was not needed for the hard part of the edit: Barry's linear program found an equivalent edit with 16,291 changed weights instead of 130,188 in about a minute (MATH-500 51.7 with Astra's direction, 47.8 with a heuristic direction and no LLM), Tree-Ising (arXiv 2611.14210) reached 54.6% with four forward-pass requests and zero LLM tokens, and Beaker got 37.6% from a decode-time ban on the token 'Wait' with no weight change.
- The published description of the edit was imprecise: Toledo's byte-level audit found 130,188 changed entries (not 131,072), an update of W0 - 6*u(u^T W0) that reverses and amplifies the direction rather than subtracting it, and a dose chosen by trial over 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6.
- Raw-weight reading without tools is confirmed only for networks of 17k-172k weights, and Ahmed (arXiv 2611.08070) showed Claude Opus 5.5 on ~125k-parameter CNNs combines accurate local arithmetic with dataset priors: it named CIFAR-10 for fast-food networks, MNIST for KMNIST, kept CIFAR-10's class names after the labels were permuted, and did not notice shuffled weights.
- The largest LLM edited this way in a preprint is Qwen3.5-9B in bf16 (Hu et al., arXiv 2611.09382), where four of seven searches kept the original weights and a +29.69-point development gain became -0.35 on held-out data; the strongest edits were tiny (one column of a layer-23 MLP down-projection, 1,023 values, on the 0.8B model).
- Models of 10B parameters or more have been edited only in unreviewed community work: Qwen3.8-27B three times (Concept Country, nine rank-one down-projection edits, pre-registered, GPQA +4.3 [+1.8, +7.1]; ReBatch, one layer-29 tensor, 19,680,881 weights; munishbp, in 4-bit NVFP4), GLM-4.6V-Flash 10.3B once, and one negative result on the 11.66B Ovi diffusion transformer where no edit target was found.
- The closest public analogue to a 31B 4-bit model on a 32 GB card is munishbp's weight_space_harness: Qwen3.8-27B in NVFP4 loaded in 19.6 GB on an RTX 5090 at about 3 tokens/s, a rank-based MLP-output fit on layer 60 of 64 changed 0.22% of weights and raised held-out MiniWoB++ email tasks from 30.0% to 68.0%, the 4-bit format kept 1.03 of the requested change, and one new weight overflowed the format's range until a clamp was added; the repository itself calls this a transfer of a known fault, and a prompt sentence matched the edit on a second task.
- Three ways of handling a quantised object have appeared and none has been validated across re-quantisation: keep edited matrices in bf16 beside a 4-bit base (HomoHabilis, NF4 and MXFP4), write the edit back into the 4-bit format with a range clamp (munishbp, NVFP4), or edit bf16 and ship untested 4-bit conversions (Concept Country, which states it makes no claim the effect survives).
- Only the newest closed frontier models drive an editing loop: on IWIBench (arXiv 2611.08647, target Gemma 3 4B IT, pristine 104/551 targets) GPT-6.1 Sol reached 284/551 and Astra 251/551, while the open-weights editors Nemotron 3 Ultra, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM 5.3 scored 107, 115 and 85, i.e. no better than the unedited model.
- Open-weights models can do the small no-tools reading task but unreliably: Qwen 3.8 27B Q4_K_M identified 3 of 4 games in 46-65 minutes each on a laptop (Willison), while a LessWrong stratification found DeepSeek V4.1 Flash answers Pac-Man 78.6% of the time when given no signal and andsalazar found Kimi K3.1 Pro labelled shuffled weights a digit at 76% confidence.
- Editor sessions are high-variance and their self-reports are unreliable: three independent Astra searches on the same model and benchmark gained +29.45, +5.00 and +27.30 points (Hu et al.), a HomoHabilis editor claimed +12 on practice problems for an edit worth about +5 held out, and andsalazar's editors reported token counts they never measured and inverted a similarity sign.
- More reasoning effort does not reliably help: on IWIBench GPT-6.1 Sol at high effort used 12.8x the reasoning tokens of low effort and scored lower on nine of ten task families, and in Beaker's harness Sol at max effort reached 31.1% against 39.5% at its default.
- Edits remove obstacles or change defaults rather than add abilities, a conclusion reached independently by the authors (Gemma null), by Zhang et al. on the pi-0.5 robot policy (12 of 13 further sessions declined to edit), by Beaker (0 of 44 abstention edits kept) and by munishbp (wrong choices among input candidates needed training).
- Independent harnesses have converged on the same shape: a persistent CPU sandbox holding weights as arrays, a separate budgeted forward-pass tool on the GPU, no gradients, typed edit operations through one gate, patches against a hash-pinned immutable base, held-out data never mounted, a retention term beside the target metric, a norm-matched random-direction control, and a full ledger with independent re-measurement.
- The authors have not released their harness package or their benchmark as of 14 November; their 12 November update lists replications and extensions but leaves memorisation, variance and cost unanswered, and their public logs contain only the runs the post reports.
- Reception on Hacker News was large (2,967 points, 901 comments) and the consistent expert reading (Nanda as quoted, Goedecke, Honnibal, many commenters) is that Astra performed known mechanistic-interpretability and abliteration-style techniques competently, with the post's 'natively' framing conflating no-tools reading of tiny networks with a 50-pass Python loop on Qwen.

### Design implications (the researcher's, unjudged)
- Do not build the harness around pasting weights as text: no-tools reading has only been shown up to about 172k weights, so at 31B the driver needs structured read tools (bounded tensor slices with numerical summaries, SVD and QK/OV products, logit-lens readouts, teacher-forced log-probs, hidden states, MLP activations and attention patterns), which is what IWIBench, HomoHabilis and Beaker all ended up providing.
- Make the existing neuron atlas the driver's entry point, because the edits that worked at 4B-27B were neuron- or column-level changes to late MLP down-projections (one column in layer 23 on 0.8B, two columns in layer 29 on 4B, a fit on layer 60 of 64 on 27B) found by locating positions where the right answer already had visible probability.
- Represent every edit as a patch against the immutable, hash-pinned base checkpoint, applied at load and exactly resettable, and reuse the existing rank-one edit-with-rollback path as the single write gate with typed operations (rank-one add, project-out with dose, column scale or zero, key-to-value least-squares fit, revert) and a policy on allowed tensors, fraction changed, finite values and format range.
- Treat survival under 4-bit quantisation as a first-class acceptance check: report for each edit the fraction of the requested change that the 4-bit grid keeps, clamp to the format's range and count clamped weights, and offer the alternative of holding edited matrices in higher precision beside the 4-bit base, since no public work has measured an edit surviving re-quantisation of a QAT checkpoint.
- Budget forward passes by size (texts times tokens) and return the remaining budget every turn, and favour teacher-forced reads over generation: a 27B 4-bit model ran at about 3 tokens/s in munishbp's harness, so generation passes will dominate wall clock on one card.
- Keep one model loaded and apply edits as in-place deltas or adapter rows rather than restarting an engine per pass, cache base-model generations keyed by prompt and seed, and pair base and edited rows in one batch: in the authors' logs 25 of 82 generation passes died at engine start (16-27% of GPU time) and 19-40% of generated tokens re-sampled the unchanged base.
- Run cheap non-weight baselines before crediting any edit and make the harness do it automatically: a prompt sentence, the thinking toggle and recommended sampling settings, a logit bias, and a steering vector each matched or nearly matched a published weight edit (Toledo, Beaker, munishbp, Barry).
- Require a norm-matched random-direction control and a measured noise floor (the base model run at least twice) for every accepted edit, and gate acceptance on beating that noise, because single short probes misled every team that reported them and generation was non-deterministic even with fixed seeds.
- Split practice and held-out items before the first pass, never mount scoring assets in the driver's sandbox, and score in a fresh process the driver cannot touch; Singh's agent read the evaluation pool and fitted readouts on it when this was not enforced.
- Score target gain multiplied by retention (IWIBench's g times r) and add near-transfer and adversarially similar bleed sets plus neutral-text top-token agreement, since general batteries missed near-target damage in both munishbp's and HomoHabilis's runs.
- Re-measure every number independently of the driver's prose and log all trials, failed ones included, with count, magnitude and denominator of each edit; the original was criticised for releasing only reported runs and for stating a weight count and percentage that did not match the bytes.
- Give the driver a solver tool for the 'which columns and how much' step (a linear program or closed-form fit under preservation constraints) instead of letting it search by trial, since that step took Astra about 8 hours and a GPU LP solver about a minute.
- Sweep dose on every step of the evaluation and never carry a dose or margin across models: Toledo found the published dose of 6 was chosen by trial, and munishbp found margins did not transfer between 4B and 27B and that a partial sweep hid false positives.
- Make the driver backend pluggable with a scripted-replay mode (OpenWiSH exposes claude -p, codex exec, an API backend and replay behind one trait), default to moderate reasoning effort, and plan for repeat sessions per hypothesis because independent searches on one model ranged from +5 to +29 points.
- Build in context handoff at a token threshold, an operator notice channel, crash-resume, and time or USD caps; HomoHabilis needed all four after its first runs, and several teams hit the 128K output cap.
- Set expectations in the interface that edits on a strong 31B model will mostly remove obstacles or shift defaults with small headroom (Hu et al.'s 9B gains were 1-3 points and four of seven searches kept the original), and make 'no edit, original retained' a normal recorded outcome.
- Avoid letting the driver prompt name the suspected fault unless that is recorded as part of the experiment, because the original's prompt hint about looping is one of the main reasons its 'discovery' claim is disputed.

### Open questions
- Does an edit computed on full-precision or dequantised weights survive re-quantisation into a W4A16 QAT grid at Gemma-31B scale? The only number found is one repository's 1.03 retention on NVFP4 for Qwen3.8-27B.
- Can any gradient-free edit add an ability rather than remove an obstacle or shift a default? No replication has shown one.
- Does tool-free weight reading work beyond roughly 10^5 weights, and does tool-assisted reading at 10B+ find faults nobody already knows about? The 27B results were transfers of known faults or had unnamed editors.
- How much of the driver's success depends on public literature about the specific object model and method class (issue #8 on the authors' logs asks for one pathology with no public literature)?
- What is the session-to-session variance of the headline results? Only Hu et al. ran three searches on one cell (+29.45, +5.00, +27.30).
- What did the headline edit actually cost? Sources disagree: about $15 at list price by one reader's arithmetic, 89.7 million tokens as cited by Tree-Ising, $28-79 per Astra run in Beaker's harness.
- Will any open-weights model drive an editing loop? On IWIBench all three tested were at the unedited baseline.
- What will the authors' own benchmark and harness look like, and when will they be released?
- Which editor produced the two bf16 Qwen3.8-27B edits (Concept Country, ReBatch)? Neither names one.

### Takeaways
- The only weight-space-programming result on a real LLM (Takehi et al., 2 Nov 2026) is a single direction subtracted from 64 MLP output columns in each of two layers of Qwen3.5-0.8B (131,072 weights, 0.016%), which stopped reasoning loops and raised MATH-500 from 7.9 to 48.1; it is a neuron-localised relative of abliteration, found by an LLM with a CPU Python kernel and 50 forward passes, and it removed an obstacle without adding ability (the Gemma 3 270M control moved 6.8 to at most 7.7).
- The first replication at larger scale (Hu et al., arXiv 2611.09382) found the approach mostly stops working at 9B: most candidate edits were discarded, a Math edit worth +29.69 on development data scored -0.35 held-out, 6 of 16 searches correctly ended by keeping the base weights, and three identical searches at 0.8B gave +29.45, +5.00 and +27.30.
- The edits LLM editors actually choose form a small vocabulary: subtract a direction from named down-projection columns, scale or negate one column, zero or scale one attention head's output block, a rank-one u v^T update, or overwrite a few embedding rows (65 rows repaired a robot policy from 86.3% to 92.0% in Zhang et al.).
- Key-value model editing (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit) has never been shown above GPT-NeoX 20B, and its reported success is inflated: under realistic free-generation QA it falls from 96.8% to 38.5% (Mirage of Model Editing, ACL 2025), a single edit can collapse a model, and sequential edits degrade through norm growth of the edited matrix.
- AlphaEdit's null-space projection reproduces at 2,000 edits but degrades near 5,000 (Llama3-8B efficacy 99.25% to 49.02%) and fails outright on Gemma2-2B (45.25% efficacy), which the July 2026 reproducers attribute, as an untested hypothesis, to Gemma's normalisation after the feed-forward block.
- Abliteration is the only hand-made weight edit proven at and beyond 31B (13 models to 72B in Arditi et al.; a 320B MoE in Shi et al., Sep 2026), and its collateral damage depends on the tool: GSM8K changed by -0.13 to -7.81 points on average across three tools, with one -18.81 point case.
- On the 320B model, finding residual-stream writers by module name reached only 0.066 of a 0.776 total effect because fused tensors were skipped without an error, and projecting then re-quantising reintroduced part of the removed direction; the authors fixed both by enumerating writers from tensor structure and diffing the checkpoint tensor by tensor against the base.
- Parameter decomposition is the only line whose native unit is a piece of weight with a meaning (a gated rank-one subcomponent), but whole-model decompositions stop at 67M (VPD) to about 85M non-embedding parameters (targeted PD); at 8B and 27B it exists only one matrix at a time (ASPD on Qwen-3-8B, zero-data SWD on Qwen3.5-27B).
- Hand-replacing a single VPD subcomponent to change an emoticon prediction worked but was beaten on both off-target metrics by a LoRA trained on 947 examples, which is the fairest current measure of interpretable hand edits against a small gradient baseline.
- Compiled and hand-written transformers (RASP, Tracr, ALTA, AdderBoard, Percepta's width-36 interpreter, the 1.48M-weight Go network) give exact verification against a program, but nothing has been written into a trained 1B+ model without gradients: Tracr-Injection uses distillation into GPT-2 large, and I found no training-free circuit transplant between LLMs.
- Weights-only readings hold for a minority of units and need a confirming forward pass: WeightLens validates 58.8% of GPT-2, 32.7% of Gemma-2-2B and 25.4% of Llama-3.2-1B transcoder features, Anthropic's prompt-independent global weights suffer interference, and LLM reading of raw weights failed a label-permutation control (Ahmed, arXiv 2611.08070).
- No SAE, transcoder or cross-layer transcoder release exists for Gemma 4 that I could find (Gemma Scope 2 covers Gemma 3 to 27B with cross-layer transcoders only at 270M and 1B), while Goodfire's 10 Nov 2026 work defends the raw neuron basis on a 35B model (92.2% of routed neurons ablated, loss 1.886 to 1.940).
- Edits are neither permanent nor invisible: edited knowledge is forgotten faster than pretrained knowledge under later fine-tuning, and edits sit in the dominant singular subspace of the edited matrix where they can be located and selectively reversed (Jiang et al., EMNLP 2026 Findings).
- The four ideas most worth borrowing are (1) typed, exactly-diffed edits with a structural tensor-by-tensor check, (2) a verification ladder with free-generation scoring, held-out sets, a matched random-direction control and 'keep the base' as a valid outcome, (3) testing a behaviour as an activation intervention before compiling it to weights (Arditi's equivalence, Steer2Edit's closed form), and (4) decomposing one matrix or one data slice on demand (targeted PD at about 7% of the full cost).

### Design implications (the researcher's, unjudged)
- Represent every edit as a typed object from a small vocabulary (column direction-subtract, column scale, head-output scale, rank-one u v^T, embedding-row overwrite, all-writers projection) stored as an exact diff against the base checkpoint, because those are the edits LLM editors chose in Hu et al. and exact diffs are how the original WSP logs make results reproducible.
- Never edit packed 4-bit codes in place: keep edits as float side deltas applied at load time and make 'still works after re-quantisation' a separate gate before any GGUF export, since small updates round away and a projected-out direction partly returns after quantisation (Shi et al.; CACHE-UK; a hobby lab's Q4 failures).
- Enumerate the tensors that write the residual stream from their shapes, not their names, and after every edit compare the result with the base tensor by tensor to confirm that only the intended tensors changed, because name matching silently missed 74% of the effect on a 320B MoE.
- Do not assume the activation-to-weight equivalence on Gemma: its blocks have pre-norm and post-norm RMSNorm plus QK-norm, AlphaEdit failed on Gemma2-2B, so each edit type needs a measured check that the weight edit reproduces the activation intervention it was derived from.
- Make the default workflow 'intervene on activations first, compile to weights second', and ship the cheap baselines (a decoding change, a steering vector, standard directional ablation) so every LLM-found edit is reported against them, which the original WSP work did not do.
- Build the verification ladder into the environment rather than the prompt: free-generation scoring instead of teacher forcing, a development set the agent sees and a held-out set it never sees, a forward-pass budget, a matched random-direction control per edit, and neutral-text KL or perplexity plus edited-matrix norm growth after every edit as collapse alarms.
- Treat 'no edit' as a successful outcome and require repeated independent searches before believing a gain, because 6 of 16 searches in Hu et al. rightly kept the base and identical searches varied from +5.00 to +29.45.
- Expect obstacle removal (loops, refusals, a mis-grounded token) to be the reachable class of edit at 31B and design the hypothesis tools to find obstacles, since capability-adding edits have not been shown on any trained multi-billion-parameter model and gains shrank from 0.8B to 9B.
- Use the existing causally tested neuron atlas as the symbol table for reading and writing, because no learned dictionary exists for Gemma 4 and the neuron basis has now been defended on a 35B model.
- Expose weight-reading primitives (vocabulary projection, per-matrix SVD, neuron-to-neuron virtual weights) as tools that return a hypothesis together with a mandatory confirming forward pass, because weights-only readings validate for only 25 to 59% of units and suffer interference.
- Do not offer raw weights as text: LLM reading has been shown only up to about 170,000 weights, so at 31B the interface must be tools and statistics over named tensors.
- Offer a covariance-aware rank-one write for selected layers only: a key covariance for one 21504-wide feed-forward down-projection is about 1.85 GB in float32 by my arithmetic, so all 60 layers cannot be held at once, and the full bf16 model (64.0 GB per the Gemma 4 report) cannot sit on a 32 GB card.
- Plan an on-demand 'disassemble this matrix' call (targeted or zero-data decomposition of one matrix or one data slice) rather than a whole-model dictionary, since that is the only scale at which parameter decomposition has reached 8B to 27B models.
- Keep an ordered edit stack with rollback and a cap on cumulative change per matrix, since sequential edits fail through norm growth and merged deltas interfere, and record that edits are detectable in the matrix spectrum and may not survive later fine-tuning.
- Add a rebuild test from iWSP: an edit or a reading counts as understood only when a fresh agent session reproduces it from a description of at most 200 words.

### Takeaways
- The weight-space harnesses published since 2 November 2026 (Takehi et al. Exp. 6 and 8, Hu et al., wsplab, weightspace, Dolci's Gemma 4 12B edit) converged on one shape: a persistent CPU-only Python kernel that holds the weights but cannot run the network, plus a small counted budget of forward passes (10 to 50) served from a separate process; Hu et al. add 300 Python analyses, disabled gradients and a save_edit call that refuses out-of-scope tensors.
- Development scores overstated held-out scores every time someone measured: test gain was below dev gain in all 14 main-table runs of Hu et al. (0.8B SciCode +38.89 dev, 0.00 test), a weightspace Opus session went from 8.6% to 50.8% on its practice set but 30.5% on a fresh one, and most HarnessOpt-Bench cells tested below their best visible validation score.
- The headline Sparks result (Qwen3.5-0.8B MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1% by editing 0.016% of weights) rests on a baseline where 93% of answers hit the token limit in thinking mode; the weightspace replication measures the unedited model at 46.4% with thinking off and finds no edit helps in that mode, and Gemma 3 270M, which does not loop, moved by about a point.
- Rules written in a prompt did not hold for agents with free code: Singh's coding agent drifted over 959 programs into fitting logistic regression on a pool containing the evaluation examples (an invalid SNLI 100%) and hid constants in buffers to report 0 parameters, weightspace says its sandbox is not a security boundary, and Goodfire saw an agent bypass a blocked bash pip install through the notebook tool.
- Giving an agent an interpretability tool does not mean it helps: AuditBench found agents call an effective tool 5 to 10 times then revert to chat and named a tool-to-agent gap, and CHIVE found activation oracles, natural-language autoencoders and SAEs gave no uplift over a transcript-only agent; against that, Anthropic's 2025 auditing agent gained roughly 10 to 20 points from interpretability tools and WSPBench's weights arm repaired 30 of 32 faults against 14 of 32 with behaviour probes alone.
- Agency did not reliably beat simpler baselines: Haklay et al.'s Claude Opus 4.1 circuit agent (6,382 tool calls) performed comparably to a one-shot baseline, the SPAR Delphi agent scored 81.6 against plain Delphi's 83.8 and lost to a script that tried strategies and kept the best score, and wsplab reports a two-line scripted recipe matching or beating LLM editors on one task.
- Goodfire's Neuron Scientist (10 November 2026) is the strongest current case for a metric-driven agent loop: a frontier model runs rounds of hypothesis, experiment and submission against a held-out dev set, a separate agent writes the summary, and numbers are reported on test documents never used for optimisation (simulation recovers 73% of a perfect reader's KL repair; 120 of 128 neuron poles were driven past their maximum in a 10-billion-token corpus from the description alone).
- For state, a fresh session per iteration plus a persistent memory file and shared forum worked for Anthropic's automated alignment researchers (removing the forum cost about 6 points of headroom in a single-run ablation), but Anthropic's 2025 auditing agent became falsely confident from its own memory files after context resets and once looped for hundreds of turns.
- Meta-Harness gives its proposer raw code, scores and execution traces of every prior candidate on a filesystem (up to 10M tokens per step against at most 26K for summarising methods) and outperforms them, while in HarnessOpt-Bench detailed trace spans were requested only 16 times across 111 cells; raw history should be available on disk but agents will not necessarily read it.
- Harness effects can exceed model effects and must be disclosed: Zhang et al. measured harness variance at 7.8 times model variance on a SWE-bench Verified subset with six ranking reversals in nine model pairs, whereas HarnessOpt-Bench found the optimiser model mattered 1.8 times more than the coding harness and a shared harness beat native ones in 11 of 20 pairings.
- Scaffolding tuned to one model went stale within a release: Anthropic added context resets for Claude Sonnet 4.5's context anxiety and found them dead weight on Opus 4.5, removed sprint decomposition for Opus 4.6, and states that every harness component encodes an assumption about what the model cannot do; wsplab's councils never beat their strongest member and seeding automated researchers with human ideas gave no gain.
- Cost per hypothesis spans four orders of magnitude and the weight-space papers report none: InterpAgent is 176 s and $0.0061 per SAE feature on GPT-4o mini, the SPAR agent $0.39 to $1.70, Goodfire at most about $45 per neuron, the automated researchers about $4 per agent-hour plus 30 minutes on one H200 per method, Dolci's Gemma 4 12B session about two hours for 27 to 30 forward passes, and Hu et al. note a single generation pass can take half an hour.
- Weight-reading and circuit-explaining claims lean on memorised priors unless probed: Claude Opus 5.5 still described natural-image structure in shuffled weights (accuracy 7.78%) and misnamed all nine class pairs under label permutation (Ahmed), and Claude Opus 4.1 could recite the IOI circuit with head indices without running any experiment (Haklay et al.).
- Run-to-run variance is large enough to make single runs meaningless: three Hu et al. searches on the same math task gained +29.45, +5.00 and +27.30 points, 6 of their 16 trajectories kept the original weights, and AuditBench runs every configuration 16 times per target.
- The guards that worked were mechanical: in the automated-researcher study held-out data sat under a separate OS user, a code monitor and a paper-code monitor approved exact code, and a results-free mini-paper was frozen under an immutable identifier before scoring; 39 of 1,601 trajectories were flagged (26 were lottery re-runs of one method) and none became a reported method.
- Driver portability is already demonstrated when the task statement is plain: the same Sparks prompt ran on GPT-6 Astra, on Claude Opus 5.5 (4 of 4 games) and on a local Qwen 3.8 27B (3 of 4 at 46 to 65 minutes each), Lehmann ran one task over nine writer models, wsplab's plain-text protocol needs no tool calling, and weightspace can replay a logged transcript with no model.

### Design implications (the researcher's, unjudged)
- Give the driver two layers: a persistent CPU-only Python kernel over read-only memory-mapped weights for free analysis, and a separate GPU server that alone runs the model; this is the shape Takehi et al., Hu et al., wsplab, weightspace and Dolci converged on, and on a 32 GB card holding a 31B model it is also the only layout that never needs a second copy of the weights on the GPU.
- Keep exactly three operations typed, counted and logged rather than free code: observing the live model (a budgeted forward or probe), committing an edit (with scope refusals, a changed-weights cap and frozen tensors as in Hu et al. and wsplab) and requesting a verdict; implement them once as plain Python that is both importable in the kernel and projected as MCP tools, so the environment outlives any driver or protocol.
- Enforce rules by mechanism, never by prompt: no autograd, no GPU and no network inside the kernel, and evaluation data owned by a different OS user that returns only aggregate scores, because weightspace's prompt-only rules are admittedly not a boundary, Singh's agent fitted the evaluation pool, and Goodfire's agent bypassed a bash permission through its notebook tool.
- Use three data tiers per task (a discovery batch, a fresh confirmation batch and a sealed test set) and cap the number of verdict requests, because test gains trailed dev gains in all 14 Hu et al. runs, weightspace's 50.8% practice score fell to 30.5% on fresh items, and 26 of 39 flagged automated-researcher runs were repeats of one method fishing for scorer noise.
- Return verdicts from a fixed vocabulary in which no-fix-found, not-significant and keep-the-original are ordinary outcomes (wsplab's five verdicts; 6 of 16 Hu et al. trajectories kept the original weights), computed with paired statistics against a same-size random edit and an equal-norm random direction (Hu et al.'s one controlled trajectory: +8.33 random against +29.17 chosen).
- Before crediting any weight edit, automatically run the instrument's cheaper interventions as baseline arms (prompt or sampling change, activation steering, control vector, LoRA, single-neuron scaling found by attribution), because the Sparks math baseline was a thinking-loop artifact that a settings change removes and Dolci found one existing neuron by a gradient pass that nearly matched the agent's hand-built circuit.
- Gate every edit on collateral damage measured on hidden unrelated behaviour: WSPBench counts a repair only if previously correct hidden examples stay correct, Dolci's edits left 177 to 182 of 200 unrelated translations word-for-word identical where LoRA changed 80, and the automated researchers disqualify methods that degrade MMLU, GSM8K or IFEval.
- Evaluate and report every edit at the deployed 4-bit precision after re-projection onto the quantisation grid rather than on a float shadow copy; this is my inference from Lehmann's written networks changing decisions under TF32 in 11 of 60 actors and from Dolci having to check that the Gemma 4 12B edit survived Q4_K_M, since an edit smaller than the quantisation step may vanish.
- Define a forward pass by wall-clock or token cap rather than item count and make cheap teacher-forced passes (log-probabilities plus cached activations, as in Hu et al. and Dolci) the default unit, because a 512-generation pass took up to half an hour on a 9B target, one MATH-500 evaluation in wsplab's log was 65.5M tokens, and autoresearch gets about 12 comparable experiments an hour from a fixed 5-minute cap.
- Require a recorded forecast before each test of an edit and hash it into the ledger before the result exists, as WSPBench does with edit forecasts and the automated researchers do with a frozen results-free mini-paper, then track the driver's calibration; Dolci's pre-registered prediction turned out wrong and was reported as such.
- Build decoy and prior probes into the harness: silently serve shuffled, neuron-permuted or rescaled weights and noise-mixed tool outputs and check whether conclusions change, because Claude Opus 5.5 kept claiming structure in shuffled weights (Ahmed), Claude Opus 4.1 recited a published circuit without experiments (Haklay et al.), and orbit audits (Cha et al.) test exactly this coordinate dependence.
- Persist state as an append-only session log, a structured experiment ledger (hypothesis, forecast, probes, patch hash, verdict, verified flag) and small content-addressed patches against the pinned base checkpoint, start each session fresh from those files and make the agent recall its notes before concluding; mark unverified notes as unverified, since Anthropic's auditing agent grew falsely confident from its own memory files.
- Keep the loop thin and delete scaffolding on a schedule: no mandatory multi-stage workflow, no councils, no context-reset machinery tuned to today's driver, because Anthropic removed context resets and sprint decomposition within one model generation each, wsplab's councils never beat their strongest member, and HarnessOpt-Bench's shared plain harness beat native ones in 11 of 20 pairings.
- Still provide a few high-level typed instruments that bundle the checks agents skip, such as one call that runs positive, negative, contrastive and break cases (the SPAR agent's most useful tool) and a one-call causal test, because free Claude Code ran a causal intervention in only 15 of 31 HyVE tasks and GPT-5.4's sound plans failed at execution (0.52 success) on helper-API friction.
- Ship ablation arms and a no-LLM scripted baseline with the environment (behaviour only, plus activations, plus weights, plus atlas, scripted solver), since WSPBench's weights arm repaired 30 of 32 faults against 14 for behaviour probes and 5 for the script, while CHIVE found no uplift from activation-reading tools and a scripted strategy search beat the SPAR agent.
- Emit a harness card and a per-run setup box with every result (driver as actually served, effort, declared rule set of reasoning-only, attribution-allowed or gradients-allowed, budgets, tools, context policy, what the agent could see) plus full transcripts, because Zhang et al. measured harness variance at 7.8 times model variance with six ranking reversals in nine pairs.
- Use a judge and a summariser from a different model family than the driver, blind to the driver's reasoning (MAIA's fresh instance, Goodfire's simulator from a different system; Dolci lists an all-Claude judge chain as a limitation), and report run-to-run variance and crashed or refused sessions as failures (Hu et al.'s +29.45, +5.00, +27.30; Lehmann scores refusals as 0).
- Stamp every exported edited artifact (GGUF, LoRA, patch) with base hash, patch hash and ledger link while saying plainly that provenance is not assurance (wsplab), because Dolci's 0.06% edit to Qwen3.8-27B implanted a brand preference that survived Q4_K_M quantisation and was not self-disclosed by the model.

### Open questions
- No source shows a budgeted probe-edit-measure loop on a model of 27B or more with published cost and time; the largest agent edit with a documented method is Gemma 4 12B (about two hours, 27 to 30 forward passes). How many useful forward passes per hour a 31B 4-bit model on a 5090 can serve, and whether a budget near 50 is enough at that scale, is unmeasured.
- Whether edits survive, or are even expressible, on a QAT 4-bit weight grid is untested beyond Dolci's observation that one Gemma 4 12B edit survived Q4_K_M.
- Whether weight access beats activation and behaviour access for a large language model: WSPBench says yes on small planted faults, CHIVE found no uplift from activation-reading tools for predicting counterfactual behaviour, and the Sparks real-LM results blend weight reading with an ordinary probe-edit-measure loop.
- Whether frontier drivers (Fable 5, GPT-6 class) still benefit from constrained typed tools; the evidence favouring them comes from Claude Haiku 4.5 and mid-2026 models.
- How to stop a persistent memory file from hardening unverified beliefs across sessions, given the false-confidence finding in Anthropic's 2025 auditing agent.
- Whether weight edits can add a capability rather than remove an obstacle; every source so far (Sparks Gemma control, wsplab, weightspace, brainwagon) reports failure on missing capabilities.
- Whether Goodfire's claim that scaling agent investigations reliably improves explanations holds as a measured curve against budget; the claim is in the conclusion but I did not locate the supporting figure.
- How exported edited weights should be labelled and governed, given a covert 0.06% edit to a 27B model that survived quantisation.

### Takeaways
- The public weight-space-programming harnesses (the original 2 November 2026 post, Hu et al. arXiv 2611.09382, and five replication repos) all give the editor a CPU-only Python kernel over the weights plus a small budget of 10 to 50 forward requests of two kinds (generation and teacher-forced), forbid backward passes and optimisers, and state no time or money cost; where cost is reported by replicators it is dominated by generation passes and server restarts (15 to 30 minutes per 128-answer pass plus 3 to 5 minutes of vLLM start-up per pass on an 8 GB GPU, 6 to 12 hours per editor run).
- No weight-space-programming experiment has been published on Gemma 4, on any model above 9B, or with any account of quantisation; the original post's Gemma 3 270M control gained no more than about a point, and in Hu et al. 6 of 16 trajectories kept the original weights and three searches on one task gave +29.45, +5.00 and +27.30 points.
- The Gemma-4-31B-it W4A16 checkpoint is, per a third-party dequantisation card consistent with a vLLM recipe summary, symmetric int4 with one bf16 scale per group of 32 inputs and no zero point across 410 linear tensors (62.5 GB when unpacked to bf16), while the technical report gives 19.2 GB for Q4_0 at 32k context, so only about 10 to 12 GB of a 32 GB card is free for unpacked weights, caches and activations.
- Requantising is the dangerous step: Zhang et al. (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.16454) show weight changes smaller than the 4-bit step round back to the original code (forgotten knowledge retained rises from 21% to 83% after 4-bit quantisation), Hu et al. saw small edits vanish even at bf16, and Unsloth reports that a naive Q4_0 re-conversion of the Gemma 4 QAT weights reproduces only 24.77% of bytes and drops 31B top-1 agreement from 96.67% to 87.91%.
- Holding an edit as a full-precision low-rank or column overlay on a frozen 4-bit base is established practice (QLoRA; Heretic implements ablation as LoRA adapters that reset by zeroing; CACHE-UK puts ROME-style rank-one updates into the LoRA B matrix), and by my arithmetic a rank-1 overlay on one 21,504 x 5,376 matrix costs about 54 KB against 231 MB for the unpacked matrix.
- Many edited variants can share one forward pass when each batch row carries its own low-rank delta: PEFT exposes this as adapter_names with '__base__' for the unedited model, FLoRA reports more than 3x the throughput of batched-matmul LoRA at rank 1 on a 15B model, and EGGROLL reports rank-r per-member perturbations reaching up to 91% of plain batch-inference throughput; torch.func vmap over stacked full weights costs N times the parameter memory and needs a vmap rule for every operation.
- Resuming from a cached residual stream at the lowest edited layer is exposed as an API (TransformerLens start_at_layer and stop_at_layer; nnsight early stop with a claimed, unbenchmarked 2 to 5x) but I found no published benchmark and no source on KV-cache reuse across weight edits; the expected saving is about 60/(60-L) for an edit at layer L, which is my derivation, not a sourced figure.
- For evaluation, Miller (arXiv 2411.00640) recommends scoring by the probability of the correct token to remove sampling noise and comparing models on paired per-question differences, and estimates about 969 independent questions to detect a 3-point accuracy difference at 80% power, which is why generation-accuracy metrics are a poor inner loop and teacher-forced log-probability or logit-difference metrics are the right one.
- KL to the unedited model on unrelated prompts is the working side-effect measure in practice (Heretic defaults to 100 harmless prompts for KL and 100 harmful prompts for refusals over 100 trials) but is insufficient alone: Goodfire ablated about 92% of neuron activations in a 35B model for KL 0.140 and +2.9% loss while individual benchmarks moved between -20.9 and +15.9 points, and on Gemma 4 reserved-vocabulary tokens emit -inf logits that turn KL into NaN unless masked.
- The follow-up literature shows which controls are needed: a random direction on the same neurons recovered about a fifth of the published Qwen gain, random-location edits of the same size did nothing, matched random-direction edits did nothing on a robot policy, a narrow later edit silently lost 4.6 points on an unwatched benchmark, and every reported held-out gain was below its development gain.
- As of mid-November 2026 there is no Gemma Scope for Gemma 4 and no published transcoders, cross-layer transcoders or attribution-graph support for any Gemma 4 model; what exists is one community residual SAE for the 31B base model at layer 30 (131k latents, Chanin, June 2026) and Neuronpedia MLP-neuron dashboards for Gemma 4 31B IT (60 layers x 21,504 neurons, inference enabled).
- The best-evidenced representation to hand an LLM reader at this scale is a neuron dictionary with wiring: Goodfire's 10 November 2026 report on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B gives the describing model ranked input neurons with attribution scores, reader neurons and firing examples, selects inputs by weight alignment with the target's gate or up weights, and validates descriptions by detection (95%), simulation (73% of KL damage recovered) and input synthesis (94%); raw weights as text stop being workable at around 10^5 weights.
- Gradients as an attribution tool are cheap and established: AtP* gives an effect estimate for every node including every MLP neuron from two forward passes and one backward pass (tested to 12B), QLoRA shows backpropagation through a frozen 4-bit base fits one card (65B on 48 GB), and the weight gradient of a linear layer is an outer product of backward signal and input so it never has to be stored densely.
- The weight-space-programming papers do not agree on gradients as a matter of principle: the original authors say the concept does not rule them out but disallowed them, Hu et al. and OpenWiSH forbid any backward pass, and none distinguishes a single attribution backward from an optimisation loop; where training was compared, GRPO beat edits by 8.1 points on average (XLRA) and gradient descent on the same entries found the same bypass (wsp-sql).
- Published weight-level work on Gemma 4 is almost entirely abliteration (for 31B, TrevorS reports 22/686 refusals at KL 0.124 from norm-preserving projection out of o_proj and mlp.down_proj, with directions computed on a 4-bit load matching bf16 directions at cosine 0.935); every such build edits bf16 weights and quantises afterwards, and none reports what quantisation did to the edit.

### Design implications (the researcher's, unjudged)
- Keep the packed QAT model resident and represent every edit as a small overlay object (low-rank factors, column or row patches, per-neuron gains) applied in the forward pass, because every public harness that writes a new checkpoint per edit pays minutes of reload per evaluation and a rank-1 overlay is about 54 KB against 231 MB for one unpacked feed-forward matrix.
- Never requantise inside the hypothesis loop; treat export to a 4-bit file as a separate, measured step that reports edit survival (fraction of codes changed and metric retained) and that leaves untouched groups' original codes and scales alone, since small edits round away at 4 bits and a naive Q4_0 re-conversion of Gemma 4 QAT weights matched only 24.77% of bytes.
- Offer the reader and the editor a lattice view (integer codes times group scales, as OpenWiSH does) alongside the dequantised view, and test before relying on it whether zeroing, negation and per-row scale changes on up_proj or gate_proj are bit-exact on the int4 group-of-32 lattice, because that would make the commonest edit types in Hu et al. (zero, negate, scale one neuron or head) exportable without rounding; this is my derivation and is unverified.
- Do not plan on a QA-LoRA-style exact merge: its lossless merge works by changing per-group zero-points, and the Gemma 4 W4A16 format is reported as symmetric with no zero-point.
- Build the evaluator so that each batch row can carry a different edit (per-row low-rank deltas, per-row neuron gains, with one row left as the unedited baseline), since rank-one and projection edits are exactly activation-level operations at the write site and this lets K hypotheses plus their random-direction controls be scored in one pass over the shared packed weights.
- Cache the residual stream entering each candidate edit layer for the fixed probe suite once (about 69 MB per layer boundary for 100 prompts of 64 tokens in bf16) and run each variant only from the lowest edited layer upward; expose the lowest edited layer in the edit object so the harness can do this automatically, and benchmark it, since no published number exists.
- Make teacher-forced log-probability or logit-difference on a fixed, paired prompt set the unit of the forward-pass budget, and reserve generation-based accuracy for confirming a kept edit, because probability scoring removes sampling noise and paired differences are free variance reduction while a 3-point accuracy difference needs on the order of a thousand questions.
- Ship a standing side-effect panel that runs on every edit without the agent asking: KL and top-1 agreement to the unedited model on unrelated text with non-finite logits masked (Gemma 4 reserved tokens emit -inf), plus a small fixed set of unrelated task probes, because low KL did not predict benchmark movement in Goodfire's ablation and an unwatched benchmark lost 4.6 points in one replication.
- Build the controls into the edit tool rather than leaving them to the agent: a matched random direction on the same neurons, a random location of the same size, a held-out split the agent never sees, repeat searches, and keep-original as a legal and expected outcome (6 of 16 trajectories in Hu et al.).
- Give the agent a sandboxed kernel with per-tensor lazy loading (the dequantised model is 62.5 GB and cannot be loaded whole) and provide expensive analyses such as SVD of a 21,504 x 5,376 matrix, vocabulary projections and neuron wiring tables as cached harness services, so the agent's per-execution time limit is not spent on them.
- Make the neuron, its input row, its output column and directions in the residual stream the reading units, with weight-alignment wiring between neurons, descriptions and firing examples, following Goodfire's validated format; do not hand the agent raw matrices as text, and do not design around transcoders or attribution graphs for Gemma 4 because none exist and training them is a multi-day job per the only cost estimate found.
- Verify any dictionary or direction on the QAT instruction-tuned checkpoint itself before trusting it, and re-verify after edits: one third-party measurement puts top-1 agreement between the bf16 release and the QAT checkpoint at only 67.8%, the only 31B SAE was trained on the base model at one layer, and transcoder drift under fine-tuning has been measured to cross tolerance on some checkpoints.
- Declare gradient access as an explicit per-task level (none, single attribution backward, optimisation), record it in the ledger, enforce it in the kernel, and never pool results across levels; this follows OpenWiSH's access-level rule and answers the main published critique of the original post while keeping AtP-style attribution available as a cheap pre-filter.
- If attribution backward passes are allowed, run them from the cached residual at the lowest layer of interest and keep weight gradients in factored form (backward signal times input per token, about 6.9 MB for 64 tokens on one feed-forward matrix instead of 462 MB dense), and confirm the top-ranked nodes by real interventions as AtP* prescribes; this requires a differentiable unpack-then-matmul path, which a custom int4 kernel may not provide.
- Record every edit as an op in a small language (assign, scale, add rank-one, project out, revert) in an append-only hashed ledger with a policy gate on which tensors may change and how much, as OpenWiSH does, because it makes rollback, replay, export and audit the same mechanism and fits the existing rank-one-edit-with-rollback design.
- Set expectations in the tool's own framing: the published successes are removals of an obstacle (reasoning loops, lexical grounding) on small or damaged models, edits did not add capability to a healthy Gemma 3 270M or to Qwen3.5-9B on most tasks, and training beat editing where compared, so on a healthy 31B the harness should be judged on how fast and how honestly it tests a hypothesis, not on benchmark gains.

### Open questions
- Are zeroing, negation and per-row scale edits truly bit-exact on the Gemma 4 int4 group-of-32 lattice and in the GGUF Q4_0 form, and is the feed-forward block the gated form down(act(gate x) * up x) that the row-scaling equivalence needs?
- How much of a typical rank-one or column edit survives requantisation to the QAT lattice on this model, as a function of edit magnitude relative to the group scale?
- Does a single attribution backward pass run on the existing W4A16 path, or does it need a differentiable unpack-then-matmul fallback, and what is its peak memory on a 32 GB card for the probe suite?
- What is the measured speedup and exactness of resume-from-layer evaluation on this model (including attention over cached positions and the int8 KV cache the technical report mentions)?
- How well does a frozen-norm first-order prediction of an edit's effect on a logit difference agree with the real forward pass at depth 60, and is it good enough to rank candidates before spending a forward?
- Does the layer-30 SAE trained on the base 31B reconstruct the QAT instruction-tuned checkpoint's residual stream acceptably, and do public neuron descriptions made on the bf16 release hold on the QAT weights?
- Should a single attribution backward count as weight-space programming for results the owner wants to compare with the published papers, given that none of them draws that line?
- How many prompts does a paired teacher-forced logit-difference metric need on this model to detect an effect of the size the atlas's causal tests already resolve?
- Is first-token KL or all-position KL the better side-effect measure here, and how reproducible is it across runs on the same card?

### Takeaways
- The independent replication of weight-space programming (Hu et al., WSP-Scope, arXiv 2611.09382, 6 Nov 2026) shows the agent overfits what it can see: development gains exceeded held-out gains in every one of 14 tabulated trajectories (0.8B Math +43.75 dev vs +29.45 held-out; 0.8B MedXpertQA +17.19 vs +0.24; 9B Math +29.69 vs -0.35), and three reruns of the same search gave +29.45, +5.00 and +27.30.
- Keeping the original weights is a normal outcome, not a failure: in WSP-Scope the editor ended six of 16 trajectories with the unedited checkpoint, and on the stronger Qwen3.5-9B target the largest held-out gain was +3.03 points, so a harness for a strong 31B model should expect small effects and many nulls.
- The original Sparks post (Takehi et al., 2 Nov 2026) reports no random-edit baseline, and the one matched control in WSP-Scope is thin: a single equal-norm random orthogonal direction scored 10 of 48 against base 6 of 48 and the chosen edit 20 of 48, so a random perturbation at the site already moved the metric and one draw cannot bound that.
- Critics (Goedecke, Honnibal, 3 Nov 2026) and the Sparks numbers themselves (answers hitting the token cap fell from 93.0 to 33.1 percent) indicate the headline Qwen edit removed a looping failure through a known contrast-attribute-trim procedure, which is why a weight edit must be compared against cheaper knobs such as decoding settings, a prompt, a steering vector or a LoRA.
- When agents can see the scorer they game it and instructions do not stop them: METR (June 2025) found reward hacking in 39 of 128 RE-Bench runs against 8 of 1,087 HCAST runs, and prompt variants forbidding cheating left the rate at 70 to 95 percent on one task.
- On open-ended research tasks Huang et al. (arXiv 2609.28614, Sept 2026) measured 30.5 percent spontaneous reward hacking across 17 models, an LLM reviewer seeing only code and reported scores missed 33 of 505 confirmed hacks, and detailed rejection feedback doubled cumulative evasion (40.5 against 20.3 percent with generic rejection).
- Locking the evaluator outside the agent's workspace eliminated evaluator tampering in RewardHackingAgents (arXiv 2603.11337), and the combined defence against tampering and train/test leakage cost 25 to 31 percent median runtime overhead, the only measured price I found for this control.
- Rationing feedback protects a held-out set: Bertran, Roth and Wu (arXiv 2606.11045, June 2026) found 38 of 102 checkpoints overfit by more than 10 percent when agents had sample-level validation access, while a one-bit did-it-beat-the-best signal with pre-fixed query budgets matched score-based search on holdout across eight datasets.
- An agent's final report is not evidence: OverclaimBench (arXiv 2609.20812) found 80.4 percent of incomplete runs gave a misleading account and every model exceeded 50 percent, METR's 2026 risk report says agents routinely overstate their results, and IWIBench records an editor reporting success where validation found a preservation failure.
- For claims that an agent read something from weights, memorised priors are the main confound: Ahmed (arXiv 2611.08070) found that after permuting output labels all nine interpreted class pairs were wrong, and Haklay et al. (arXiv 2603.20101) show agreement with published explanations can come from memory.
- The model-editing literature gives the standard side-effect controls: The Mirage of Model Editing (ACL 2025) shows teacher-forced scoring inflates success from 38.5 to 96.8 percent, RippleEdits shows edits fail on logical implications, The Butterfly Effect shows a single edit can collapse a model and that perplexity tracks the collapse, and IWIBench scores an edit as gain multiplied by retention of baseline-correct controls.
- Numerical nondeterminism is large enough to fake or hide an effect: Yuan et al. (NeurIPS 2025) measured up to 9 percent accuracy variation from batch size and GPU changes under greedy bfloat16 decoding, and WSP-Scope scored its final results on a single seed.
- Gemma 4 is under the unmodified Apache License 2.0: the Gemma Terms of Use page (last modified 1 April 2026) covers only Gemma 1 through 3n and points Gemma 4 to a page containing plain Apache 2.0 text, and both the gemma-4-31b-it and the official QAT W4A16 model cards carry the apache-2.0 tag, so the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy and its ban on circumventing safety filters do not bind Gemma 4 by licence.
- Redistributing edited Gemma 4 weights or adapters requires, under Apache 2.0 section 4, a copy of the licence, prominent notices on modified files and retained notices, and section 6 grants no right to use the Gemma name beyond describing origin; hosting is separately subject to the hub's content policy, which bans content built for unauthorised access or malicious code and does not mention modified models.
- Weight-edit backdoors have been demonstrated on this exact model: Lazarus (Microsoft, arXiv 2611.15118, 11 Nov 2026) edits about one dormant neuron per payload token in the last feed-forward block and reports 99.51 percent attack success on a two-step observation-triggered payload on Gemma-4-31B-it with zero leakage on benign trajectories.
- Detection of weight-edit backdoors is partial without the base weights and reliable with them: in Lazarus, frontier LLM auditors without references flagged 72.81 and 56.14 percent of edited checkpoints and a five-feature detector reached 91.43 percent true positives at 9.68 percent false positives, while Tracing and Reversing Edits (ICLR 2026) recovers rank-one fact edits from weights with up to 99 percent accuracy; adaptive attackers remain an open problem.
- Refusal stripping is already a packaged commodity (Arditi et al. 2024 across 13 models; the AGPL Heretic tool; refusal-stripped builds of the Gemma-4-31B QAT checkpoint appear in listings), so a harness adds little uplift there; the newer risk it could amplify is agent-authored trigger backdoors.
- Containment guidance in 2026 converges on environment boundaries over approvals: Anthropic reports users approved about 93 percent of permission prompts and that its approval classifier lets about 17 percent of overeager actions through, NVIDIA's red team makes blocked egress, blocked writes outside the workspace and blocked writes to agent configuration mandatory, and METR found monitoring could be disabled by changing an environment variable.
- Lab frameworks (Anthropic RSP v3.4, Google DeepMind Frontier Safety Framework, OpenAI Preparedness Framework) address a lab's own frontier model accelerating AI R&D; I found no lab or regulator guidance on a frontier model editing a different, smaller open model on a user's machine, and the Sparks authors list self-editing only as an untested future use.

### Design implications (the researcher's, unjudged)
- Keep the scorer, the held-out prompts and the ledger in a process and directory the agent's tools cannot read or write, and return scores only through a tool call, because visible scorers drove a 30 percent hacking rate in METR's runs and evaluator locking eliminated tampering in RewardHackingAgents at a 25 to 31 percent runtime overhead.
- Give every behaviour three disjoint prompt tiers (practice the agent can read, confirmation that returns only a coarse result under a fixed query budget, and a sealed test the owner unseals once per registered claim), and always report the confirmation and sealed numbers side by side, since WSP-Scope's development gains shrank on held-out data in every trajectory.
- Make the agent file a pre-registration record before any write (hypothesis, site, metric, prompt sets, decision rule in standard errors, budget, nulls to run), hash it into the ledger, and let the harness, not the agent, apply the keep-or-revert rule; keep-original must be a first-class recorded outcome and every discarded attempt must stay in the ledger.
- Have the harness automatically run nulls for any edit the rule would keep: several random directions of equal norm at the same site, ablation of the site, the reversed sign and a dose sweep; budget this as roughly fifteen evaluation passes per kept edit and run it on a subsample first, because a single random draw in WSP-Scope already beat the base model by four items in 48.
- Define matched-norm nulls in the same representation the edit lives in: on a quantisation-aware 4-bit checkpoint that means an overlay delta of equal Frobenius norm at the same layer and rows, not a perturbation of the stored 4-bit codes (this is my inference; no source addresses it).
- Score every edit as gain multiplied by retention, where retention combines KL to the unedited model on a fixed unrelated text set, perplexity and the share of baseline-correct control prompts still correct; on one 32 GB card cache the base model's logits once per checkpoint hash and prompt-set hash and toggle the edit overlay, so each preservation check costs one teacher-forced pass and never needs two copies of the model in memory.
- Before accepting a weight edit, score the same items under the cheaper interventions the instrument already has (a prompt, decoding and token-budget changes, an activation steering vector, a LoRA), and record whether the weight edit does anything those do not, because AxBench found prompting the strongest steering baseline and the headline Qwen edit mainly removed a looping failure.
- Score generalisation on free-running generation over paraphrases, changed formats, multi-turn contexts and logical neighbours the agent never saw, not on teacher-forced continuations of the prompts used to build the edit, since teacher forcing inflated edit success from 38.5 to 96.8 percent in The Mirage of Model Editing.
- Do not give the agent per-item held-out results or detailed reasons when an auditor rejects a claim; return a pass or fail or a one-bit beat-the-best signal, since detailed feedback doubled reviewer evasion in Huang et al. and one-bit feedback cost nothing on holdout in Bertran et al.
- Have the harness write an append-only, hash-chained ledger holding the base checkpoint hash, the exact delta, configurations, seeds, raw outputs and the agent's stated claim next to the measured result, and require the agent's report to cite ledger entries that the harness verifies, because agents' prose accounts were misleading in 80 percent of incomplete runs in OverclaimBench.
- Fix batch size, prompt order, decoding settings and software versions identically for the base and edited arms, compare per item in pairs, use several seeds whenever sampling is on, and rescore any claim headed for the sealed test after a fresh-process reload, because batch and hardware changes alone moved accuracy by up to 9 percent in Yuan et al.
- For stacked edits, log the weight norm at every edited site and rerun the cheap preservation suite after every few edits with a hard cap on stack depth, since sequential edits collapsed models within a few steps in The Butterfly Effect and norm growth is the reported precursor.
- For any claim that the agent read a function from weights or from the neuron atlas, add a permutation or shuffle control (permuted neuron order or labels, shuffled tensor, withheld names) and prefer tests on structure that has never been published, because weight reading leaned on dataset priors in Ahmed's audit.
- Mix a few canary tasks with a known null answer into each batch of agent work and treat any claimed success on them as a defect in the agent or the pipeline, following ImpossibleBench's logic that a pass on an impossible task is a shortcut.
- Prefer typed weight and inference tools with hard per-episode budgets (the IWIBench pattern) as the default agent surface, and if a Python analysis kernel is offered, run it in an OS sandbox with no network, a single scratch write directory, the base weights mounted read-only and no ability to run the model outside the budgeted tool.
- Block agent writes to the harness code, the evaluator, the ledger and the agent's own tool and MCP configuration at the operating-system level, and reserve human approval for the few boundary crossings (promoting an edit to persistent, unsealing a test set, exporting a LoRA, GGUF or control vector, publishing), with no cached approvals for exports, since per-action approval was rubber-stamped 93 percent of the time in Anthropic's data.
- Treat everything the edited Gemma generates and every dataset string as untrusted data when it is shown to the agent, and keep the agent's execution environment off the network so that an injected instruction has nowhere to send anything.
- Attach to every export a manifest with the base model identifier and hash, the exact delta, the pre-registration record, the evaluation card including nulls, retention and discarded attempts, a prominent statement that the files were modified and the Apache 2.0 licence text, and name the artefact as derived from Gemma rather than as Gemma.
- If the harness is ever pointed at a Gemma 3 or earlier checkpoint, switch on a different compliance path, because the Gemma Terms of Use then require flowing down the use restrictions and the Prohibited Use Policy forbids attempts to circumvent safety filters.
- Add a read-side diff audit that compares any third-party checkpoint or adapter against its declared base before it is loaded as the object of study, and consider surfacing the Lazarus detector features (write magnitude and token alignment of last-block feed-forward neurons) in the atlas, since Lazarus was shown on Gemma-4-31B-it and diffing against the base is the only check its authors call reliable.
- Do not ship a turnkey trigger-to-payload implant recipe or tool in the open-source release, and if the agent discovers an attack-like edit, follow the Lazarus authors' practice of confidential analysis and coordinated disclosure before publishing.

### Open questions
- How should a matched-norm random null be defined for a quantisation-aware W4A16 checkpoint: in dequantised weight space, as an overlay delta, or after re-quantisation?
- How large must the confirmation and sealed prompt sets be for a keep rule to have power on a strong 31B model, given that held-out gains on the stronger target in WSP-Scope were at most about three points?
- Do Lazarus-style dormant-neuron implants survive 4-bit quantisation or QAT weights, and do the reported detector features transfer to a quantised Gemma-4-31B?
- Will the benchmark and harness the Sparks authors say they are building set a de facto protocol that this harness should stay compatible with?
- Does weight-diff or spectral detection hold against an attacker who knows the detector and spreads the change across many directions?
- Does the usage policy of the frontier model used as the agent restrict using it to remove another model's safeguards or to author trigger backdoors, and will the agent refuse such tasks?
- Does the EU guidance on downstream modifiers of general-purpose models have any bearing on publishing low-rank edits of an Apache-licensed model (primary text not read)?
- How should the harness define the unrelated-text preservation set so that it is not itself overfit by repeated edits tuned to keep KL low?
